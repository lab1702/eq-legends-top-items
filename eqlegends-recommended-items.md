# EverQuest Legends — Community-Recommended Items

*Compiled August 1, 2026, and wiki-checked the same day. EQL launched July 28, 2026, so most of this is beta-era and launch-week community knowledge. Where a claim has been checked against the wiki it says so and links the page; everything else is a starting point, not gospel. See Caveats for what was verified and what's still open.*

**Sources:** [EQL Wiki](https://eqlwiki.com), [EQ Legends Tools](https://eqlegendstools.com), [EverQuest Guides farm list](https://www.everquestguides.com), AllThingsHow launch guide.

## Why people chase these

EQL's **Exaltation** system is the reason this list looks the way it does. You can level an item (+1 through +10), then extract its click, proc, or focus effect and socket it into a different item you actually want to wear. So a mediocre wand with a great click is worth farming purely as a donor. Two rules trip people up: you must be able to *equip* the donor item with one of your three classes, and both the donor and destination need to share a class or the mote is wasted.

**How far you have to level a donor depends on the effect type, not on the item.** The [Exaltations wiki page](https://eqlwiki.com/Exaltations) gives four separate thresholds — a **focus** effect comes out at **+1**, a **click** at **+2**, a **worn** effect at **+3**, and a **proc** at **+4**.

This resolves the "+2 or +4?" argument you'll see in chat — both camps were right about different effect types. Practical consequence: a focus donor is cheap (one level), a proc donor is the expensive case. Don't budget +4 across the board or you'll overpay by three levels on every focus item.

Two further wiki-documented rules on the destination side: an Exaltation carries **the equipment slot restriction of its source item** (so a two-hander's proc lands in Primary only), and once inserted, the destination item's usable classes **narrow to the overlap** with the Exaltation's classes.

Higher difficulty tiers don't change the loot table — they change merge value. D0 = 1 point, D1 = 2, D2 = 4, D3 = 8, D4 = 16. A D4 drop is worth sixteen D0 drops when pushing toward +10.

## Best Clickies and Focus Effects (the ones that follow you to 50)

These are the "farm the effect, not the item" picks. Level each to the threshold for its effect type before extracting — **+1** for a focus, **+2** for a click, **+4** for a proc (see above). Most of this section is focus and click effects, so the majority are cheaper donors than the old "budget +4 for everything" advice implied.

## Instant-Clicky Tricks Worth Knowing

The reason so many instant clickies show up on best-in-slot lists isn't the effect — it's the **global cooldown reset**. Normally you wait ~1.5 seconds after a spell before starting another. Clicking an instant-cast item immediately after a spell lets you begin the next cast right away.

- **Crowd control** — more roots, stuns, and mez per second, critical when charm breaks.
- **Cannibalize (shaman)** — canni → clicky → canni, faster than clicking nothing (though Canni has its own built-in cooldown unless it's in gem slot 1).
- **Quadding** — flux-staff between nukes to get back to medding sooner.
- **Buffing** — clicky between each buff saves ~9 seconds on a full rotation.
- **Caveat:** to chain-cast the *same* spell it must be in gem slot 1. Slots 2–8 won't repeat. Bards don't experience global cooldown at all.

## Caveats

- **Last checked against the wiki: August 1, 2026.** That pass verified the Exaltation extraction thresholds, the three legacy-item flags, the Journeyman's Boots drop question, and the Ignite damage figure — and corrected the doc on the last three. It also filled in real sources for what was then a **Utility Clickies** section, where the whole column previously just said "Drops"; two of those turned out to be epic questlines rather than drops, the section's old "cheap to get" label didn't survive, and in the end so little of it remained that its last two items were folded into Best Clickies and the section retired. A follow-up sweep for thin entries then fixed the four worst: the **Short Sword of the Ykesha** was under a wrong name, **Runed Bone Fork** and **Earthshaker** had no location at all, and **Footman's Blade** turns out to have no wiki page under that name — that one is now flagged rather than patched. **Seven items were then removed as unreachable.** Five were sourced from Out-of-Era zones — the Bracer of the Hidden and Goblin Gazughi Ring (Lake of Ill Omen), the Shrunken Goblin Skull Earring (Warsliks Woods), the Lodizal Shell Boots (Iceclad Ocean), and the Fungus Covered Great Staff (Old Sebilis). Two more went when epics turned out to be Out of Era as well: **Black Fur Boots** (Shaman Epic reward) and **SoulFire** (its only routes being the Paladin epic and a "Zimel's Blades" quest that has no wiki page at all). Ghoulbane survived, but only via the Upper Guk camp — its quest route is out. All of these are worth knowing about whenever the content lands; none belongs in a list you farm from today. Quests confirmed still in era: the Journeyman's Boots quest and Lynuga's Gem Collection. The most recent patch on the wiki at time of checking was the **2026-07-29 hotfix**. **Everything not named in these Caveats is still launch-week community knowledge that nobody has re-checked.** Four claims remain open and need someone in-game rather than another source: whether Drelzna actually drops JBoots, whether An Evil Eye still drops the Manastone, whether the Guise has any route at all, and what Ignite hits an ordinary mob for.
- **This game is four days old** (launched July 28, 2026). Item stats, drop rates, and whether legacy items still drop are all in flux. The EQL Wiki is explicitly in beta and flags several items as "no longer drops" — and as below, those flags don't all mean the same thing.
- **This list is Classic-era only, on purpose.** EQL launched with Classic content and the Planes; the wiki's News page says to "assume that Kunark and Velious content will not be present immediately," and zone pages mark those expansions **"Out of Era"** — which means *not currently accessible*, not merely high-level. That label is easy to misread, and item pages for Kunark and Velious gear look completely normal, so **an item page with real stats and a real drop source is not evidence you can go get it.** Check the era first. Anything sourced from Out-of-Era content has been removed from this document (see the note below).
- **Class epics are Out of Era too, and that gate is separate from zones.** The [Class Epic Quest List](https://eqlwiki.com/Class_Epic_Quest_List) is headed "Epic Quests Era: Out of Era," and individual epic pages carry the same flag — the [Paladin Epic](https://eqlwiki.com/Paladin_Epic_Quest), for instance, whose zones are *all* Classic (Plane of Fear, Plane of Hate, Kaladim, The Hole, Freeport, Nektulos, Felwithe, Erudin). **So a quest can be gated even when every zone it touches is open.** Checking the zone is not enough; check the quest page's own era flag. This also takes out non-epic-looking quests that sit under the epic umbrella, like **The Sword of Nobility** (the Ghoulbane quest).
- **"Legacy item" covers two different situations, and people say it as if it were one.** Checked 2026-08-01: the Guise of the Deceiver has **no documented source of any kind** (not dropped, not sold, no quest, not crafted), while the Manastone merely has **no confirmed sightings** with its drop source still listed. Neither is settled. Treat "it doesn't drop anymore" as a claim needing a source, not a fact.
- **Watch for classic-EQ answers to EQL questions.** Searching any legacy item name returns Project 1999, Allakhazam, Project Quarm, and 1999-era usenet threads at the *top* of the results. They look authoritative and describe a different game. The tell is a date or the phrase "on Live" — the EQL wiki itself inherits some of these notes, which is how a page can list a 20% drop rate and a "made a quest in 1999" note at the same time (see Journeyman's Boots).
- **Wiki pages are demonstrably incomplete, so absence of information isn't evidence.** Concrete example: the Ignite page lists only two weapons carrying the proc and omits the Shimmering Ruby Stiletto, which its own item page confirms has it. When a page doesn't mention a source, that's as likely to be a gap as a fact.
- **Three different sites call themselves the EQL wiki** — `eqlwiki.com`, `eqlegends.wiki`, and `everquestlegends.wiki`. Only the first is the one this document treats as tier 1. The others appear to be content farms that recycle each other, so agreement between them is not independent confirmation of anything.
- **Beta wiped July 21**, so everything above is a launch target, not something people currently have banked.
- **Procs were broken through most of beta** and got fixed July 14. The widely-repeated "Ignite lands small, ~8 a hit" figure is **probably an artifact of how it was measured.** The [wiki](https://eqlwiki.com/Ignite) lists Ignite at **38 (L8) to 46 (L16)**, resist type Fire, and the scaling caps at L16 — so a level-40 character should see ~46, not 8. The one parse behind the low number was taken on a **Lord Nagafen** kill, i.e. a fire proc against a level-55 fire dragon, which is close to the worst possible target for it. Note the wiki does *not* actually document Nagafen's fire resistance (the "fire and magic resists mean everything" line on his page is about *your* resists surviving Lava Breath), so this is a strong hypothesis rather than a confirmed one. Either way, don't treat 8 as Ignite's damage — it needs one parse against an ordinary non-fire mob.
- **Motes stopped being tradable July 14** — everyone farms their own.
- Rule of thumb for all of it: **D0/D1 for leveling, D4 for farming**, non-respawning instance for a clean clear.
